The specific part of maturita topic 13 (Geography and environment), at B2 level: the geography of the UK, its nations, people and political system. London and the sights are in [London and Places of Interest in the UK](/aj/realie/london-and-places-of-interest-in-the-uk/).

## The name and the countries

The official name is **the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland**. It is made up of four countries – **England**, **Scotland**, **Wales** and **Northern Ireland** – each with its own capital, traditions and national symbols.

| Country | Capital | Patron saint and symbol |
| --- | --- | --- |
| England | London | St George, the red rose |
| Scotland | Edinburgh | St Andrew, the thistle |
| Wales | Cardiff | St David, the daffodil and the leek |
| Northern Ireland | Belfast | St Patrick, the shamrock |

It is important not to mix up the names: **Great Britain** is the largest island (England, Scotland and Wales), **the UK** also includes Northern Ireland, and **the British Isles** is a geographical term for Great Britain, the whole island of Ireland and more than 6,000 smaller islands, such as the Hebrides, the Orkneys and the Shetlands.

## Geography

The UK lies in north-western Europe, separated from the continent by the **English Channel** and the **North Sea**; since 1994 it has been connected with France by the **Channel Tunnel**. It covers about 243,000 km², which is roughly three times the size of the Czech Republic. Its only land border is with the Republic of Ireland.

- **Mountains:** the north and west are mountainous – the **Scottish Highlands** with **Ben Nevis (1,345 m)**, the highest mountain in the UK, the **Pennines** (the "backbone of England"), the **Lake District** with the Cumbrian Mountains, and **Snowdonia** in Wales. The south and east are lower, with rolling hills and flat land; the lowest area is the **Fens** in eastern England.
- **Rivers and lakes:** the rivers are short but deep. The longest is the **Severn**, followed by the **Thames**, which flows through London. The largest lake is **Lough Neagh** in Northern Ireland; Scotland is famous for its lochs, such as **Loch Ness** and **Loch Lomond**.
- **Coast:** no place in the UK is more than about 120 km from the sea. The coastline is long and varied, from the white chalk **cliffs of Dover** to the rocky Scottish coast.

## Climate and weather

The climate is **mild** and **changeable**. Thanks to the warm **Gulf Stream** and the Atlantic Ocean, winters are mild and summers rarely very hot. The weather can change several times a day, which is why the British talk about it so much. The west is wetter than the east, and Scotland is colder than the south of England.

## People and languages

The UK has about **69 million** inhabitants, most of whom live in England and in big cities such as London, Birmingham, Manchester, Glasgow and Liverpool. It is a **multicultural** society – many people have their roots in former British colonies, for example in India, Pakistan or the Caribbean. The official language is **English**, spoken with many regional accents; **Welsh** is spoken by about a fifth of the population of Wales and **Scottish Gaelic** in parts of Scotland.

## Political system

The UK is a **constitutional monarchy** and a **parliamentary democracy**. It has no single written constitution; the rules are based on laws, traditions and court decisions.

- **The monarch** – **King Charles III** (since 2022; his heir is **Prince William**) is the head of state, but his role is mostly ceremonial: he opens Parliament, signs laws (the Royal Assent) and represents the country. He is also the head of the **Commonwealth** and of the Church of England.
- **Parliament** in the Palace of Westminster has two chambers: the **House of Commons** with **650 Members of Parliament** (MPs), elected at least every five years, and the **House of Lords**, whose members are not elected – most of them are appointed for life.
- **The government** is formed by the party that wins the majority in the House of Commons. Its leader becomes the **Prime Minister**, who lives and works at **10 Downing Street**. The second largest party forms the **Opposition** with its "shadow cabinet".
- Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their **own parliaments or assemblies** which decide on things like education and health.

The UK was a member of the European Union from 1973 until **Brexit** in 2020. It is still a member of NATO and one of the five permanent members of the UN Security Council.

## Symbols

- **The flag** – the **Union Jack** combines the crosses of St George (England), St Andrew (Scotland) and St Patrick (Ireland). Wales is not represented on it.
- **The national anthem** – *God Save the King*.
- **The currency** – the **pound sterling** (£), divided into 100 pence.

## Economy

The UK was the first industrial country in the world – the **Industrial Revolution** started there in the 18th century. Today most people work in **services**: London is one of the world's biggest **financial centres**, and the country is strong in research, education, media, creative industries and tourism. Industry produces cars, aircraft parts, chemicals and medicines, and there is oil and gas under the North Sea. Agriculture is small but efficient – sheep, cattle and cereals.
