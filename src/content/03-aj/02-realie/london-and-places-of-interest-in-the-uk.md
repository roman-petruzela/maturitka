## London

### Basic facts

London is the capital of England and of the whole UK and, with about **9 million** people, one of the largest cities in Europe. It lies in the south-east of England on the **River Thames**. It is the seat of the **King**, **Parliament** and the **government**, one of the world's leading **financial centres** and a very **cosmopolitan** city – over 300 languages are spoken there.

### A short history

- **AD 43** – the Romans founded a settlement called **Londinium** at a place where they could cross the Thames.
- **1066** – after the Norman Conquest, William the Conqueror started building the **Tower of London**.
- **1666** – the **Great Fire of London** destroyed most of the medieval city. The architect **Sir Christopher Wren** then rebuilt many churches, including **St Paul's Cathedral**.
- **19th century** – under Queen Victoria London became the capital of the British Empire and the largest city in the world; in **1863** the first **underground railway** in the world was opened.
- **1940–1941** – during the **Blitz**, German bombing destroyed large parts of the city.
- **2012** – London hosted the **Olympic Games** for the third time.

### The main parts of the city

- **The City** (the "Square Mile") – the historic centre and the financial district, full of banks and modern skyscrapers such as the Gherkin and the Shard.
- **Westminster** – the political centre with Parliament, government offices and Westminster Abbey.
- **The West End** – theatres, musicals, shopping streets, restaurants and nightlife.
- **The East End** – traditionally a working-class area, today a lively multicultural district.

### Sights

- **The Houses of Parliament** (the Palace of Westminster) with the clock tower known as **Big Ben** – strictly speaking, Big Ben is the name of the great bell; the tower has been called the Elizabeth Tower since 2012.
- **Westminster Abbey** – a Gothic church where English and British monarchs have been crowned since 1066, most recently King Charles III in 2023, and where many famous people are buried.
- **Buckingham Palace** – the official London residence of the monarch, famous for the **Changing of the Guard**.
- **The Tower of London** – a medieval fortress that has served as a royal palace, a prison and a place of execution. Today it houses the **Crown Jewels** and is guarded by the **Beefeaters**. Legend has it that if the **ravens** ever leave the Tower, the kingdom will fall.
- **Tower Bridge** – a Victorian bridge whose middle part can be raised to let ships through.
- **St Paul's Cathedral** – Wren's Baroque masterpiece with a huge dome.
- **Trafalgar Square** with **Nelson's Column**, commemorating the Battle of Trafalgar (1805), and the **National Gallery**.
- **Piccadilly Circus** – a busy junction famous for its neon advertising and the statue known as Eros.
- **The London Eye** – a giant observation wheel on the South Bank with a great view of the city.
- **Museums and galleries** – most of them are free: the **British Museum**, the **Natural History Museum**, the **Science Museum**, the **Victoria and Albert Museum** and **Tate Modern**, a gallery of modern art in a former power station.
- **Parks** – **Hyde Park** with **Speakers' Corner**, where anyone can make a speech on any subject, St James's Park, Regent's Park and Kensington Gardens.
- **Markets and shopping** – Oxford Street, Covent Garden, Camden Market and Harrods department store.
- **Greenwich** – the **Prime Meridian** (0° longitude) runs through the Royal Observatory here.

### Getting around

The **Underground**, known as **the Tube**, has eleven lines and is the oldest underground railway in the world. Other symbols of London are the red **double-decker buses** and the black **cabs**, whose drivers have to pass a famous exam called "the Knowledge". You pay with a contactless card or an **Oyster card**. London has six airports, the largest of which is **Heathrow**.

## Places of interest in the UK

### England

- **Stonehenge** – a circle of huge standing stones built around 5,000 years ago. Nobody knows for sure what it was used for, but it may have been a temple or a kind of calendar.
- **Oxford** and **Cambridge** – the two oldest English universities (from the 12th and 13th centuries), made up of dozens of colleges. Their students compete in the famous **Boat Race** on the Thames.
- **Stratford-upon-Avon** – the birthplace of **William Shakespeare**.
- **Bath** – a spa town founded by the Romans, with Roman baths and elegant Georgian streets; the novelist Jane Austen lived there.
- **Canterbury** – the seat of the Archbishop of Canterbury, the head of the Church of England, with a magnificent cathedral.
- **York** – a medieval city with city walls and **York Minster**, one of the largest Gothic cathedrals in northern Europe.
- **Liverpool** – a port city famous for **the Beatles** and football; **Manchester** – a former textile centre and the home of two famous football clubs.
- **The Lake District** – a national park in north-west England with lakes and mountains, which inspired Romantic poets such as William Wordsworth.
- **Hadrian's Wall** – a Roman wall built across northern England in the 2nd century to protect the province from northern tribes.
- **The white cliffs of Dover** on the south coast, the first thing travellers see when crossing the English Channel.

### Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland

- **Edinburgh** – the capital of Scotland, with its castle on an extinct volcano and the Royal Mile. Every August it hosts the **Edinburgh Festival** and the **Fringe**, the world's largest arts festival.
- **The Scottish Highlands** – wild mountains, valleys and lochs, including **Loch Ness** with its legendary monster, and **Ben Nevis**, the highest mountain in the UK.
- **Wales** – called "the land of castles", with Snowdonia National Park and the capital **Cardiff**.
- **Northern Ireland** – the **Giant's Causeway**, about 40,000 hexagonal basalt columns on the coast, and the capital **Belfast**, where the Titanic was built.
