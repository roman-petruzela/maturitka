The specific part of maturita topic 2 (Personal identification and characteristics), at B2 level. As with Czech personalities, you don't have to know everyone – pick a few people from different fields and be ready to describe what they were like and why they are important. The words for describing personality from topic 2 are very useful here.

## History and politics

- **Elizabeth I (1533–1603)** – the queen of England during the "Golden Age". Under her rule England defeated the Spanish Armada (1588) and theatre flourished. She never married and was called **the Virgin Queen**.
- **Queen Victoria (1819–1901)** – she reigned for 63 years, when Britain was the strongest power in the world and the **British Empire** covered a quarter of the globe.
- **Winston Churchill (1874–1965)** – Prime Minister during the **Second World War**, famous for his determination and speeches ("We shall never surrender"). He also won the Nobel Prize for Literature.
- **Elizabeth II (1926–2022)** – the longest-reigning British monarch (70 years), respected for her sense of duty.
- **George Washington (1732–1799)** – the commander of the American army in the War of Independence and the **first President** of the USA.
- **Abraham Lincoln (1809–1865)** – the 16th President; he led the country through the **Civil War** and **abolished slavery**. He was assassinated in a theatre.
- **Martin Luther King Jr. (1929–1968)** – a Baptist minister and leader of the **civil rights movement** who fought against racial segregation by **non-violent** means. He won the Nobel Peace Prize and was murdered in 1968.

## Literature

- **William Shakespeare (1564–1616)** – the greatest English playwright and poet, born in Stratford-upon-Avon. He wrote tragedies (*Hamlet*, *Romeo and Juliet*, *Macbeth*), comedies (*A Midsummer Night's Dream*) and sonnets, and he invented many words and phrases still used today.
- **Jane Austen (1775–1817)** – the author of *Pride and Prejudice*; her novels describe love, marriage and money among the English middle class with gentle irony.
- **Charles Dickens (1812–1870)** – the best-known Victorian novelist. In *Oliver Twist* and *A Christmas Carol* he showed the poverty of children and the poor in industrial London.
- **Oscar Wilde (1854–1900)** – an Irish writer famous for his wit, the comedy *The Importance of Being Earnest* and the novel *The Picture of Dorian Gray*. He was sent to prison for his homosexuality.
- **George Orwell (1903–1950)** – the author of *Animal Farm* and *Nineteen Eighty-Four*, warnings against dictatorship; words like "Big Brother" come from him.
- **J. K. Rowling** – the author of the **Harry Potter** series, one of the best-selling books in history.
- **Edgar Allan Poe (1809–1849)** – an American master of horror stories and the inventor of the detective story (*The Murders in the Rue Morgue*, *The Raven*).
- **Mark Twain (1835–1910)** – the author of *The Adventures of Tom Sawyer* and *Huckleberry Finn*, set on the Mississippi.
- **Ernest Hemingway (1899–1961)** – a Nobel Prize winner known for his short, simple sentences (*The Old Man and the Sea*, *A Farewell to Arms*).

## Science and technology

- **Isaac Newton (1643–1727)** – formulated the **law of gravity** and the laws of motion; according to legend he was inspired by a falling apple.
- **Charles Darwin (1809–1882)** – explained the **theory of evolution** by natural selection in *On the Origin of Species*.
- **Thomas Edison (1847–1931)** – an American inventor with over a thousand patents, including the practical **light bulb** and the phonograph.
- **Alan Turing (1912–1954)** – a mathematician who helped to **break the German Enigma code** in the Second World War and is considered the father of **computer science** and artificial intelligence. He was persecuted for being gay and was officially pardoned only in 2013.
- **Tim Berners-Lee** – a British computer scientist who invented the **World Wide Web** in 1989 and gave it to the world for free.
- **Stephen Hawking (1942–2018)** – a physicist who studied black holes and wrote *A Brief History of Time*, although he was almost completely paralysed by a serious illness.
- **Bill Gates** and **Steve Jobs (1955–2011)** – the founders of **Microsoft** and **Apple**, who made personal computers available to ordinary people.

## Music, film and sport

- **The Beatles** – the most successful band in history, from Liverpool.
- **Charlie Chaplin (1889–1977)** – a London-born star of silent films, famous as the little tramp with a bowler hat and a cane.
- **Freddie Mercury**, **Elvis Presley**, **Bob Dylan** (who won the Nobel Prize for Literature), **Adele** and **Taylor Swift** in music.
- **Muhammad Ali**, **Michael Jordan**, **Serena Williams** and **David Beckham** in sport.

## Describing a person

Use the vocabulary of topic 2 to describe what a person was **like**, not only what they did:

- **Appearance:** *He was tall and slim, with…*
- **Character:** *She was determined, hard-working and brave, but also…*
- **Achievements:** *He is best known for…*
- **Influence:** *Thanks to him / her, today we…*
- **Your opinion:** *What I admire most about her is…*

## Useful phrases

- *He / She is regarded as one of the greatest…*
- *He / She will always be remembered for…*
- *Not only did he…, but he also…*
- *If it hadn't been for him / her, we wouldn't have…*
- *The person I find most inspiring is…, because…*
