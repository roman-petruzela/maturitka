## Public holidays

On public holidays most people have a day off and many shops are closed, especially at Christmas and Easter. Some holidays are religious, others remind us of important moments of Czech history.

| Date | Holiday | What it commemorates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 January | New Year's Day / **Restoration Day of the Independent Czech State** | the birth of the Czech Republic in 1993 |
| March / April | **Good Friday** and **Easter Monday** | Easter |
| 1 May | **Labour Day** | workers' rights; also the day of love |
| 8 May | **Liberation Day** | the end of the Second World War in Europe in 1945 |
| 5 July | **Saints Cyril and Methodius Day** | the arrival of the two missionaries in Great Moravia in 863 |
| 6 July | **Jan Hus Day** | the burning of Jan Hus in 1415 |
| 28 September | **St Wenceslas Day** (Czech Statehood Day) | the murder of the patron saint of the Czech lands in 935 |
| 28 October | **Independent Czechoslovak State Day** | the founding of Czechoslovakia in 1918 – the most important national holiday |
| 17 November | **Struggle for Freedom and Democracy Day** | the student protests of 1939 and the start of the Velvet Revolution in 1989 |
| 24–26 December | **Christmas Eve**, **Christmas Day** and **St Stephen's Day** | Christmas |

## Christmas

Christmas is the most important family holiday of the year. The four Sundays before it form **Advent**, when people decorate their homes, light candles on an **Advent wreath** and visit Christmas markets.

On **5 December**, on the eve of St Nicholas Day, children meet **St Nicholas** (Mikuláš), an **angel** and a **devil** in the streets. Good children get sweets and fruit, while naughty ones are – jokingly – threatened by the devil and get a lump of coal or a potato.

The main day is **Christmas Eve (24 December)**. Some people fast all day, because, according to tradition, they will see a **golden piglet** in the evening. The traditional dinner consists of **fish soup** and **fried carp with potato salad**; the carp is often bought alive a few days before and kept in the bathtub. After dinner the family finds presents under the **Christmas tree** – they are brought by **Baby Jesus** (Ježíšek), not by Father Christmas. People also bake many kinds of **Christmas biscuits**, and there are old customs that are supposed to tell the future: cutting an apple in half (a star in the middle means health), floating walnut shells with candles in water, or girls throwing a shoe over their shoulder to find out whether they will get married. Many people go to **midnight mass**.

## Easter

Easter is a spring festival with Christian as well as pagan roots. Its symbols are **painted eggs** (kraslice), **lambs** baked from sweet dough and spring flowers. On **Easter Monday** boys and men go from house to house with a **pomlázka**, a whip plaited from willow twigs, and gently whip girls and women, which is supposed to keep them healthy and young. In return they get painted eggs, sweets or a shot of alcohol. In some parts of Moravia girls pour cold water on the boys the next day. Today many people, especially in cities, see this custom as outdated.

## Other traditions

- **Masopust** – the Czech carnival before Lent, with masks, parades and pig slaughters (zabijačka) in villages.
- **Burning of the witches** (pálení čarodějnic) on **30 April** – people make bonfires and burn a straw witch to celebrate the end of winter.
- **1 May** – it is a tradition for couples to kiss under a blossoming cherry tree, and young people visit the statue of the Romantic poet Karel Hynek Mácha in Prague.
- **Name days** (svátky) – almost every day of the year belongs to a name, and Czechs celebrate their name day almost like a second birthday.
- **All Souls' Day** (2 November) – people visit cemeteries and light candles on the graves of their relatives.
- **New Year's Eve** (Silvestr) – parties, fireworks at midnight and lentil soup on New Year's Day, which is supposed to bring money.

## Festivals and events

Apart from traditional holidays, there are many modern festivals: the **Karlovy Vary International Film Festival**, the **Prague Spring** music festival, open-air music festivals such as **Rock for People** or **Colours of Ostrava**, folklore festivals in Moravia, wine festivals in autumn and Christmas markets in every town.

- *The most important holiday for my family is…, because…*
- *It is a tradition / It is customary to…*
- *According to tradition, …*
- *We usually spend Christmas Eve…*
- *What I like most about it is…*
- *Unlike in Britain, Czech children get their presents…*
- *Some people think this custom is outdated, but…*
