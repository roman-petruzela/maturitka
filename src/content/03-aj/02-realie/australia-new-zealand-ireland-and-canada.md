The specific part of maturita topic 14 (Climate and weather, fauna and flora), at B2 level: the geography, climate and weather of four English-speaking countries and how they compare. Each country starts with a short profile; the comparison table and the typical questions are at the end.

## Australia

- **Basic facts:** the only country that is also a **continent**; about 7.7 million km² and about 27 million people. The capital is **Canberra**, the largest cities are **Sydney** and **Melbourne**. It has six states and two territories. It is a constitutional monarchy – King Charles III is the head of state, represented by the Governor-General.
- **Geography:** most of the interior, called **the Outback**, is flat, dry **desert** or semi-desert, with the famous red rock **Uluru** (Ayers Rock), a sacred place of the Aboriginal people. The **Great Dividing Range** runs along the east coast; its highest peak, **Mount Kosciuszko** (2,228 m), is the highest point of the continent. Off the north-east coast lies the **Great Barrier Reef**, the largest coral reef system in the world, which can even be seen from space. The longest river is the **Murray**.
- **Climate and weather:** **tropical** in the north, with a wet and a dry season; **desert** in the centre; **temperate** in the south-east and south-west, where most people live. The **seasons are reversed** – Christmas is in summer. Australia often suffers from **droughts**, **heatwaves** and **bushfires**, which are getting worse because of climate change.
- **Fauna and flora:** Australia is famous for unique animals, especially **marsupials**, which carry their young in a pouch – the **kangaroo**, the **koala**, which eats only eucalyptus leaves, and the **wombat**. Other typical animals are the **platypus**, a mammal that lays eggs, the **emu**, a big bird that cannot fly, and many dangerous snakes, spiders and crocodiles. The most common tree is the **eucalyptus**.

## New Zealand

- **Basic facts:** two main islands, the **North Island** and the **South Island**, about 2,000 km south-east of Australia; about 270,000 km² and 5.3 million people. The capital is **Wellington**, the largest city **Auckland**. The original inhabitants are the **Māori**; the Māori language and New Zealand Sign Language are official languages, while English is used in everyday life.
- **Geography:** the country lies on the border of two tectonic plates, so there are **earthquakes** and **volcanoes**. The North Island has active volcanoes, hot springs and **geysers** around **Rotorua**, and the largest lake, **Taupo**. The South Island is covered by the **Southern Alps**, with **Aoraki / Mount Cook** (3,724 m), glaciers and deep **fjords** such as Milford Sound. The landscapes were made famous by *The Lord of the Rings* films.
- **Climate and weather:** **mild** and **temperate**, influenced by the ocean, with plenty of rain, especially on the west coast. Winters are cool rather than cold, except in the mountains, and the weather can change quickly.
- **Fauna and flora:** because the islands were isolated for millions of years, about 80 % of the plants and many animals live nowhere else. The national symbol is the **kiwi**, a small brown bird that cannot fly – New Zealanders call themselves "Kiwis". There were no native land mammals apart from bats, but there are millions of **sheep** – several for every person.

## Ireland

- **Basic facts:** the island west of Great Britain is divided into the **Republic of Ireland** (about 70,000 km², about 5.4 million people, capital **Dublin**) and **Northern Ireland**, which is part of the UK. The Republic is a member of the EU and uses the euro; its official languages are **Irish (Gaelic)** and English.
- **Geography:** a flat, green centre with lakes, bogs and fields surrounded by low mountains along the coast. The highest mountain is **Carrauntoohil** (1,039 m), the longest river the **Shannon**. The west coast is rocky and wild, with the **Cliffs of Moher** and the Wild Atlantic Way.
- **Climate and weather:** a **mild, wet oceanic** climate influenced by the Atlantic and the **Gulf Stream** – no great heat, no hard frost, but a lot of rain and wind, sometimes several kinds of weather in one day. That is why the country is so green and is called **the Emerald Isle**.
- **Fauna and flora:** green meadows full of cattle and sheep, peat bogs and wild flowers. According to legend, **St Patrick** drove all the snakes out of Ireland – in fact there have been none since the Ice Age. The national symbol is the **shamrock**.

## Canada

- **Basic facts:** the **second largest country** in the world (almost 10 million km²), but with only about 41 million people, most of whom live within a few hundred kilometres of the border with the USA. The capital is **Ottawa**, the largest city **Toronto**; other important cities are Montreal and Vancouver. It has ten provinces and three territories, and two official languages – **English** and **French**, which is spoken mainly in **Quebec**. It is a constitutional monarchy with King Charles III as head of state.
- **Geography:** the **Rocky Mountains** in the west, with **Mount Logan** (5,959 m) in the Yukon as the highest peak; huge **prairies** in the middle; the rocky **Canadian Shield** with thousands of lakes and forests; and the Arctic islands in the north. Canada has the **longest coastline** in the world and more lakes than any other country, including part of the **Great Lakes**. The **Niagara Falls** are on the border with the USA; the Canadian part is called the Horseshoe Falls.
- **Climate and weather:** mostly **continental**, with long, very **cold winters** and short, warm summers; the far north is **arctic**. The west coast around **Vancouver** is much milder and rainier thanks to the Pacific Ocean. Snowstorms and severe frost are common – that is why ice hockey is the national winter sport.
- **Fauna and flora:** huge **forests** of conifers and maples – the **maple leaf** is on the flag – and wild animals such as the **beaver** (the national animal), the **moose**, **grizzly** and **polar bears**, wolves and caribou.
