## Geography

The Czech Republic lies in the heart of Central Europe. It is a **landlocked** country which borders Germany in the west and north-west, Poland in the north, Slovakia in the east and Austria in the south. It consists of three historical lands – **Bohemia** in the west, **Moravia** in the east and a small part of **Silesia** in the north-east – and it is divided into fourteen regions, one of which is the capital, Prague.

Bohemia is surrounded by mountain ranges, which form most of the border: the **Krkonoše** (Giant Mountains) with **Sněžka (1,603 m)**, the highest peak of the country, the Ore Mountains, the Bohemian Forest (**Šumava**) and others. In the east lie the **Beskydy** and the **Jeseníky**. Inside the country there are rolling hills, valleys and fertile lowlands along the big rivers.

The country is sometimes called **the roof of Europe**, because its rivers flow into three different seas: the **Vltava** (430 km, the longest Czech river) flows into the **Elbe**, which empties into the North Sea, the **Morava** flows into the Danube and the Black Sea, and the **Odra** into the Baltic Sea. There are many artificial lakes and dams, especially on the Vltava (Lipno, Orlík, Slapy), and thousands of fishponds in South Bohemia.

The climate is **temperate**, with four seasons: warm and sometimes hot summers and cold winters with snow, especially in the mountains.

## People and language

Most of the inhabitants are Czechs; the largest minorities are **Ukrainians**, Slovaks and Vietnamese. Czech is a Slavic language, closely related to Slovak, and famous for difficult sounds such as **ř**. A large part of the population does not belong to any church, which makes the Czech Republic one of the least religious countries in Europe.

## Political system

The Czech Republic is a **parliamentary democracy**. Power is divided into three branches:

- **Legislative** – **Parliament** has two chambers: the **Chamber of Deputies** (200 members elected for four years) and the **Senate** (81 senators elected for six years; a third of them every two years). Parliament passes laws, and the Chamber of Deputies can pass a vote of no confidence in the government.
- **Executive** – the **government** led by the **Prime Minister**, who is usually the leader of the strongest party after the elections, and the **President**. Since 2013 the president has been **elected directly** by the citizens for five years and can serve no more than two terms in a row. The president represents the country abroad, appoints the Prime Minister and judges, is the commander-in-chief of the army, can veto laws and grant pardons.
- **Judicial** – independent **courts**, with the **Constitutional Court** in Brno protecting the constitution.

Every citizen over **18** has the right to vote.

**State symbols:** the **flag** (a white and a red horizontal stripe with a blue triangle), the **coat of arms** with the white two-tailed **lion** of Bohemia, the **national anthem** *Kde domov můj* ("Where Is My Home"), originally a song from a 19th-century play, and the linden (lime tree) as the national tree.

## Economy

The Czech lands were among the most industrial parts of Austria-Hungary. After the communist period (1948–1989), when the economy was controlled by the state, the country went back to a **market economy** in the 1990s. Today the economy is **export-based** and closely connected with Germany.

- **Industry:** the most important branch is the **car industry** – Škoda Auto in Mladá Boleslav, Hyundai in Nošovice and Toyota in Kolín – together with engineering, electronics, chemicals and the traditional **glass** and porcelain industry.
- **Energy:** coal-fired power stations in the north, being gradually closed, and two **nuclear power plants**, Temelín and Dukovany.
- **Agriculture:** wheat, barley, sugar beet, potatoes and **hops** for the famous Czech beer; vineyards in South Moravia.
- **Services and tourism** employ more and more people, and the country also has a growing IT sector.

## History in brief

- **9th–10th century** – Great Moravia, the first Slavic state; the Přemyslid dynasty and St Wenceslas, the patron saint of the Czech lands.
- **14th century** – the golden age under **Charles IV**, King of Bohemia and Holy Roman Emperor.
- **15th century** – the reformer **Jan Hus** was burnt at the stake in 1415, which led to the Hussite wars.
- **1620** – the defeat at White Mountain; the Czech lands were ruled by the Habsburgs for the next three hundred years.
- **19th century** – the **National Revival** of the Czech language and culture.
- **28 October 1918** – the founding of **Czechoslovakia**, with T. G. Masaryk as its first president.
- **1938–1945** – the Munich Agreement and the Nazi occupation.
- **1948** – the communist coup; **1968** – the Prague Spring crushed by the Warsaw Pact armies.
- **17 November 1989** – the **Velvet Revolution**; Václav Havel became president.
- **1 January 1993** – the peaceful **split of Czechoslovakia** into the Czech Republic and Slovakia.
