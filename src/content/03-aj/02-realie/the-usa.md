The USA is a **federal republic** of **50 states** and the **District of Columbia**, where the capital lies. Forty-eight states form one block of land between Canada in the north and Mexico in the south; **Alaska** lies in the north-west of the continent, and **Hawaii** is a group of volcanic islands in the Pacific Ocean.

## Geography

The country stretches from the **Atlantic Ocean** in the east to the **Pacific Ocean** in the west, and the Gulf of Mexico lies to the south. It is often divided into regions – **New England** in the north-east, the **South**, the **Midwest**, the **Southwest** and the **West**.

- **Mountains:** the old and low **Appalachians** run along the east coast. Almost the whole western half is covered by the **Rocky Mountains** and other ranges such as the **Sierra Nevada**. The highest peak, **Denali** in Alaska (6,190 m), was renamed Mount McKinley again by the federal government in 2025. Between the mountains lie deserts and plateaus, and **Death Valley** in California is the lowest, hottest and driest place in North America.
- **Plains:** between the Appalachians and the Rockies lie the huge **Great Plains** and the Central Lowlands – the "breadbasket" of America, where wheat and corn are grown.
- **Rivers:** the **Mississippi** and its tributary the **Missouri** form one of the longest river systems in the world. Other important rivers are the **Colorado**, which created the Grand Canyon, the **Rio Grande** on the border with Mexico, and the **Hudson**, which flows into the Atlantic in New York.
- **Lakes:** the five **Great Lakes** – Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie and Ontario – lie on the border with Canada and form the largest group of freshwater lakes on Earth. The **Niagara Falls** are on the river between Lake Erie and Lake Ontario. The **Great Salt Lake** in Utah is saltier than the ocean.

## Climate

Because the country is so large, it has almost every type of climate: **arctic** in Alaska, **continental** in the Midwest with hot summers and very cold winters, **desert** in the Southwest, **mild and rainy** in the north-west and **subtropical** to tropical in Florida and Hawaii. The USA often suffers from natural disasters – **hurricanes** on the Gulf and Atlantic coasts, **tornadoes** in the Great Plains ("Tornado Alley"), **earthquakes** and **wildfires** in California, and floods along the big rivers.

## People

The USA is a **nation of immigrants**, often described as a **melting pot** or, more recently, a "salad bowl", because different groups keep their own cultures. The original inhabitants were **Native Americans**; then came European settlers, Africans brought as slaves, and later immigrants from all over the world. Today about a fifth of Americans are **Hispanic** (of Latin American origin), and there are large African American and Asian American communities.

## Political system

The **Constitution** of 1787 is the oldest written national constitution still in use. It divides power into three branches:

- **Legislative** – **Congress** in the **Capitol** has two chambers: the **Senate** (100 senators, two for each state, elected for six years) and the **House of Representatives** (435 members, elected for two years; bigger states have more of them).
- **Executive** – the **President**, who is both head of state and head of government, commander-in-chief and can veto laws. He or she is elected for four years by the **Electoral College** and can serve only **two terms**. The President lives and works in the **White House**.
- **Judicial** – the **Supreme Court** with nine judges appointed for life, who decide whether laws agree with the Constitution.

The two main parties are the **Democrats** (symbol: the donkey) and the **Republicans** (symbol: the elephant). Each state also has its own government, laws and taxes.

**Symbols:** the flag, **the Stars and Stripes**, has 13 stripes for the original colonies and 50 stars for today's states; the national anthem is *The Star-Spangled Banner*; the national bird is the **bald eagle**.

## Natural wonders

The USA has more than 60 **national parks**. The best known are:

- **Yellowstone** (Wyoming) – the first national park in the world (1872), with hot springs, bison, bears and the geyser **Old Faithful**, which erupts regularly.
- **The Grand Canyon** (Arizona) – a canyon about 1.8 km deep, cut by the Colorado River over millions of years.
- **Yosemite** (California) – granite rocks such as El Capitan, waterfalls and giant sequoias.
- **Sequoia National Park** – home of the **General Sherman Tree**, the largest living tree on Earth by volume.
- **The Everglades** (Florida) – a huge wetland with alligators and crocodiles.
- **Monument Valley** and **Arches** – red rock formations known from westerns.
- **Mount Rushmore** (South Dakota) – the faces of four presidents – Washington, Jefferson, Theodore Roosevelt and Lincoln – carved into a mountain.
