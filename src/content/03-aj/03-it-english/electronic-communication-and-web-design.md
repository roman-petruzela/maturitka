The specific part of maturita topic 19 (Media and communication), at B2 level: how people communicate electronically at work and in private, and the basic rules of good web design.

## Ways of electronic communication

| Way | Typical use | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **email** | formal and work communication, attachments | permanent record, the receiver can answer later | slow for quick questions, spam and phishing |
| **instant messaging** (WhatsApp, Messenger, Signal) | quick private messages, group chats | fast, cheap, photos and voice messages | interrupts work, misunderstandings |
| **team chat** (Slack, Microsoft Teams) | communication in companies and schools | channels for topics, file sharing, integration with other tools | too many notifications |
| **video calls** (Zoom, Teams, Google Meet) | meetings, online lessons, job interviews | face-to-face contact at a distance | "Zoom fatigue", technical problems |
| **social networks** | sharing with friends or the public | reaching many people | privacy, fake news |
| **forums, comments** | discussions, help with problems (Stack Overflow, Reddit) | knowledge of many people | trolls, unreliable answers |
| **SMS and phone calls** | urgent things, two-factor codes | work without the internet / immediate | cost, no record (calls) |

### Writing a professional email

- A clear **subject line**: *Request for a meeting – project X*.
- A suitable **greeting**: *Dear Mr Smith* / *Dear Sir or Madam* (formal), *Hi Tom* (informal).
- A short **opening**: *I am writing to ask about…*
- The **main message** in short paragraphs; one email = one topic.
- A polite **closing** and **sign-off**: *I look forward to hearing from you. Kind regards / Best regards*, your name and contact details (**signature**).
- Check spelling and attachments before you click "send"; use **CC** (copy) and **BCC** (hidden copy) carefully.

### Security

Watch out for **phishing** – fake emails or messages that try to make you click a link or give your password or card number. Typical signs are urgency ("Your account will be blocked!"), mistakes, a strange sender address and unexpected attachments. Use **end-to-end encrypted** apps for private communication and never share passwords.

## Rules for designing webpages

### Content and structure

- Know your **target audience** and the **aim** of the website.
- Put the most important information at the top; users **scan** pages rather than read them.
- Use **headings**, short paragraphs and lists; write simply.
- Make the **navigation** clear and the same on every page; the logo should lead to the home page.
- Every page needs a clear **call to action** – what should the user do next (buy, contact, sign up)?

### Design

- **Keep it simple** – white space is not wasted space.
- Use a limited **colour scheme** (two or three main colours) and good **contrast** between text and background.
- Use at most two **fonts**, readable on screens and big enough (at least 16 px for body text).
- Keep the **layout consistent** – the same style of buttons, menus and headings.
- Use high-quality, optimised **images** with **alt text**.

### Technical rules

- **Responsive design** – the page must work on mobile phones, tablets and computers; today most people browse on phones (**mobile first**).
- **Speed** – compress images, minimise code; slow pages lose visitors.
- **Accessibility** – the site should also be usable by people with disabilities: alt text for images, keyboard navigation, sufficient contrast, correct HTML structure (WCAG guidelines).
- **Valid code** – HTML for structure, CSS for appearance, JavaScript for behaviour, kept separate.
- **SEO** – a meaningful title and description, headings and clean URLs help search engines.
- **Security and law** – HTTPS, a cookie consent banner and a privacy policy (GDPR).
- **Test** the site in different browsers and on different devices.
