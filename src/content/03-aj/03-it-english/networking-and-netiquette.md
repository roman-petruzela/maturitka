The specific part of maturita topic 1 (Family and interpersonal relationships), at B2 level. "Networking" has two meanings that are both useful here: **computer networks** that connect devices, and **social networking** – building relationships online. Netiquette is the set of rules for behaving well on the internet.

## Computer networks

A **network** is two or more devices connected so that they can share data and resources such as files, printers or an internet connection.

**Types of networks by size:**

| Type | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PAN** (personal area network) | devices around one person | a phone connected to wireless headphones by Bluetooth |
| **LAN** (local area network) | one building or campus | a school or office network |
| **WLAN** (wireless LAN) | a LAN using radio waves | Wi-Fi at home |
| **MAN** (metropolitan area network) | a city | a network connecting city offices |
| **WAN** (wide area network) | a country or the whole world | **the internet** – the largest WAN |

**Hardware:** each device needs a **network interface card** (NIC) or a wireless adapter. Devices are connected by **cables** (twisted pair, optical fibre) or **wirelessly**. A **switch** connects devices within a LAN and sends data only to the device it is meant for; a **router** connects different networks – for example your home network with the internet – and chooses the best route for the data; an **access point** provides Wi-Fi; a **modem** connects you to your internet service provider (ISP). A **firewall** filters traffic and protects the network from attacks.

**Topologies** describe how devices are arranged – **star** (all devices connected to a central switch – the most common today), **bus**, **ring** and **mesh**.

**How data travels:** data is split into small **packets**, which are sent separately and put together again at the destination. Every device has an **IP address** (for example 192.168.1.10 in IPv4, or a much longer IPv6 address), and rules called **protocols** – such as **TCP/IP**, **HTTP(S)** for websites or **DNS**, which translates domain names into IP addresses – make sure that devices understand each other.

**Client–server vs. peer-to-peer:** in a client–server network, computers (clients) ask a central **server** for services such as files, email or websites. In a **peer-to-peer** network, all computers are equal and share resources directly.

## Social networking

**Social networks** such as Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, X or LinkedIn let people stay in touch with family and friends, share photos and opinions, find people with the same interests and build professional contacts. **LinkedIn** is a social network for professional networking, where people present their skills and look for jobs.

**Advantages:** keeping in touch with people who live far away, finding information and communities quickly, making new friends, promoting a business or your own work.

**Disadvantages:** **addiction** and time-wasting, **cyberbullying**, **fake news**, comparing yourself with the perfect lives of others, privacy problems – the platforms collect huge amounts of **personal data** – and the risk of meeting people who are not who they say they are.

## Netiquette

**Netiquette** (net + etiquette) is a set of unwritten rules for polite and safe behaviour online:

1. **Remember the human** – on the other side of the screen there is a real person. Don't write anything you wouldn't say to someone's face.
2. **DON'T WRITE IN CAPITAL LETTERS** – it looks like shouting.
3. **Respect other people's privacy** – don't share photos or private messages of others without their permission.
4. **Think before you post** – the internet never forgets, and future employers may see your posts.
5. **Don't spread rumours or fake news** – check information before you share it.
6. **Don't feed the trolls** – ignore people who only want to provoke others.
7. **Use the right tone** – a formal email to a teacher or employer is different from a chat with a friend.
8. **Respect copyright** – don't use other people's texts, photos or music as your own.
9. **Keep messages short and clear**, and don't send spam or chain messages.
10. **Protect yourself** – use strong passwords and two-factor authentication, and never share your passwords or personal details with strangers.

## Online relationships and family

Technology has changed family life: families keep in touch through group chats and video calls, but sometimes they sit at one table, each looking at their own phone. Parents worry about the time their children spend online and use **parental controls**, while young people often help their grandparents to use smartphones. A healthy approach is to set rules – for example no phones during meals – and to balance online and offline relationships.
