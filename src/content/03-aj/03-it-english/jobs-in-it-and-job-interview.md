The specific part of maturita topic 10 (Jobs and occupations), at B2 level: the most common jobs in information technology, what you need to get them, and how to succeed in a job interview – including the questions you are likely to hear.

## Jobs in IT

| Job | What the person does |
| --- | --- |
| **software developer / programmer** | designs, writes and tests programs and apps; can be **front-end** (what users see), **back-end** (servers, databases) or **full-stack** (both) |
| **web developer / web designer** | creates websites; a designer is responsible for the look and **user experience** (UX/UI) |
| **tester / QA engineer** | looks for bugs and checks that software works as it should |
| **system administrator** | installs and maintains servers, computers and user accounts, makes backups |
| **network administrator / engineer** | designs and runs networks – routers, switches, Wi-Fi, firewalls |
| **IT support / helpdesk technician** | helps users with problems with hardware and software – often the first job after school |
| **cybersecurity specialist** | protects systems and data from attacks and tests them for weaknesses |
| **database administrator** | designs and looks after databases |
| **data analyst / data scientist** | analyses large amounts of data and finds patterns; works with statistics and AI |
| **DevOps / cloud engineer** | automates the deployment of software and runs it in the cloud |
| **project manager** | plans projects, leads a team, communicates with the customer and watches the deadline and budget |
| **game developer**, **graphic designer**, **IT teacher**… | |

### Pros and cons of working in IT

**Pros:** high demand and good salaries, interesting and creative work, possibility to work **remotely** or abroad, flexible working hours, many career paths.

**Cons:** long hours in front of a screen and little movement, stress and deadlines, the need to keep learning all the time because technology changes fast, and pressure from AI tools, which take over some simple tasks.

### What employers want

- **Hard skills** – programming languages, networks, databases, operating systems, **certificates** (e.g. Cisco, Microsoft, AWS).
- **Soft skills** – teamwork, communication, problem-solving, time management, the ability to learn.
- **English** – documentation, forums and communication in international teams are in English.
- **A portfolio** – your own projects, for example on GitHub, often matter more than grades.

## Applying for a job

1. Find a **job advertisement** (on job portals, LinkedIn or company websites).
2. Send a **CV** (BrE) / **résumé** (AmE) and a **cover letter** – a short letter explaining why you want the job and why you are the right person.
3. A good CV is short (one or two pages), clear and true. It contains personal details, **education**, **work experience** (part-time jobs and internships count), **skills**, languages, certificates and hobbies.

## The job interview

### Before the interview

- Find out as much as you can about the company.
- Read the job description again and prepare examples of your skills.
- Dress appropriately and arrive on time – or test your camera and microphone for an online interview.

### During the interview

- Greet the interviewer, shake hands and keep eye contact.
- Listen carefully, answer honestly and give **concrete examples**.
- Don't criticise your previous employer or school.
- At the end, ask one or two questions yourself – it shows interest.

### Typical questions

| Question | Tip |
| --- | --- |
| *Tell me about yourself.* | Two minutes: education, experience, skills, what you are looking for – not your whole life. |
| *Why do you want to work for us?* | Show that you know the company and connect it with your interests. |
| *What are your strengths and weaknesses?* | Give a real weakness and say how you are working on it. |
| *Describe a problem you solved.* | Use the **STAR** method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. |
| *Where do you see yourself in five years?* | Show ambition that fits the company. |
| *Why should we hire you?* | Sum up how your skills match the job. |
| *What salary do you expect?* | Find out the usual salary for the position beforehand. |
| *Do you have any questions for us?* | Ask about the team, training or the first tasks – not only about holidays. |

## Useful phrases

- *I'm a hard-working and reliable person who…*
- *I have experience with… / I'm familiar with…*
- *During my internship at…, I was responsible for…*
- *One of my strengths is… On the other hand, I sometimes…, so I'm trying to…*
- *I'd like to work for your company because…*
- *Could you tell me more about…?*
