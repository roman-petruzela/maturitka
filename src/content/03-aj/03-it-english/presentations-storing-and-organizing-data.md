The specific part of maturita topic 9 (Learning and education), at B2 level: how to give a good presentation, and how to store, organise and protect data – two skills every student and IT worker needs.

## Giving a presentation

### Preparation

1. **Know your audience and your aim** – who will listen and what they should remember.
2. **Collect and check information** from reliable sources and note them down.
3. **Make an outline** – introduction, main part with three or four key points, conclusion.
4. **Prepare the slides** in a program such as **PowerPoint**, **Google Slides**, **Keynote** or **Canva**.
5. **Rehearse** – practise aloud, check the timing and try the equipment (projector, laptop, clicker) before you start.

### Good slides

- **Less is more** – one idea per slide, key words instead of full sentences (the "6×6 rule": no more than six lines with six words).
- A clear, readable **font** that is big enough, and good **contrast** between text and background.
- **Pictures, charts and diagrams** instead of long text; always mention the source.
- A consistent **design** – the same template, colours and fonts.
- Animations and transitions only if they help; too many distract the audience.

### Delivering it

- Start with something that catches attention – a question, a surprising fact or a short story – and say what you are going to talk about (**signposting**).
- **Don't read from the slides**; speak freely and use short notes.
- Keep **eye contact**, speak slowly and clearly and use your voice and **body language**.
- Finish with a short **summary** and leave time for **questions**.

## Storing data

### Where we keep data

- **Local storage** – the internal drive of your computer or phone (HDD or SSD).
- **External storage** – USB flash drives, external hard drives, memory cards.
- **Network storage** – a school or company server, or a **NAS** (network-attached storage) at home.
- **Cloud storage** – services such as **Google Drive**, **OneDrive**, **iCloud** or **Dropbox** keep your files on servers on the internet, so you can reach them from any device, share them and work on them with others at the same time.

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| local / external | fast, works without the internet, under your control | can be lost, stolen or damaged |
| cloud | access from anywhere, sharing, automatic backup | needs the internet, monthly fee for more space, privacy depends on the provider |

### Backing up

A **backup** is a copy of your data which you can use when the original is lost because of a broken disk, a stolen laptop, a mistake or **ransomware**. A well-known rule is **3-2-1**: keep **3** copies of your data, on **2** different types of media, and **1** of them in another place (for example in the cloud). Backups should be **automatic** and you should test that you can really restore them.

## Organising data

- **Folders** – create a logical structure, for example *School → 2026 → English → Presentations*, instead of saving everything on the desktop.
- **File names** – short, clear and consistent, without spaces or special characters, often with a date: *2026-03-15_english_presentation_v2.pptx*.
- **File formats** – documents (.docx, .pdf, .odt), spreadsheets (.xlsx, .csv), images (.jpg, .png), videos (.mp4), archives (.zip). **PDF** is best for sharing a document that should look the same everywhere.
- **Versions** – keep track of versions, or use tools that save them automatically (cloud services, **Git** for programmers).
- **Databases** – large amounts of structured data are stored in **tables** in a database (for example MySQL or Access) and searched with a query language such as **SQL**.
- **Tags and search** – modern systems let you add tags and find files by searching for their content.

## Protecting data

Use **strong, unique passwords** and a **password manager**, turn on **two-factor authentication**, **encrypt** sensitive data and laptops, install updates and antivirus software, and be careful about what you share. In the EU, personal data is protected by the **GDPR**.

## Useful phrases for presentations

- *Today I'd like to talk to you about…*
- *My presentation is divided into three parts. First,… Then,… Finally,…*
- *Let's move on to… / This brings me to my next point.*
- *As you can see in this chart, …*
- *To sum up, … / Thank you for your attention. Are there any questions?*
