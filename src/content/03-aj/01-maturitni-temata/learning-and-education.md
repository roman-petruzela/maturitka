Maturita topic 9, general part, at Cambridge B2 First level. The specific part of this topic is [Presentations, Storing and Organizing Data](/aj/it-english/presentations-storing-and-organizing-data/).

## What to talk about

- your school – subjects, teachers, what you like and dislike
- the education system in the Czech Republic compared with the UK and the USA
- ways of learning – at school, online, lifelong learning, learning languages
- exams, marks and pressure
- your plans after the school-leaving exam

## Model answer

I'm in my final year at a **secondary technical school**, where I study information technology. Apart from **general subjects** like Czech, English and maths, we have a lot of **specialised subjects** – programming, networks, databases and hardware. My favourite subject is programming, because I like solving problems and seeing that my code works. On the other hand, I've never been good at physics. What I appreciate about our school is that the **practical lessons** are well equipped and some of our teachers have experience from IT companies.

The **Czech education system** starts with **nursery school**, which is compulsory for one year before primary school. **Compulsory education** lasts nine years at a **basic school**. Then students choose between a **grammar school**, which prepares them for university, a **secondary technical school** like mine, or a **vocational school** where they learn a trade. Secondary education usually ends with the **maturita**, the school-leaving exam, which is necessary for university studies. Most universities in the Czech Republic are public, and studying at them is **free of charge**.

The British system is different. Children start school at the age of four or five, and at sixteen they take **GCSE** exams in many subjects. Those who want to go to university continue for two more years and take **A-levels**, usually in only three or four subjects. Most British pupils wear **school uniforms**, and university **tuition fees** are high. In the USA, students go to **high school** until eighteen, and university, or **college**, is also very expensive.

I think the way we learn is changing. Thanks to the internet we can learn almost anything online – from video tutorials and online courses to apps for learning languages. I learnt a lot of my English from films and games. But I still believe that a good teacher who can **motivate** students can't be replaced. In the future, **lifelong learning** will be necessary, especially in IT, where knowledge becomes **out of date** very quickly.

After the maturita I'd like to **apply to** a technical university and **major in** computer science. If I don't get in, I'll start working and study part-time.

## Grammar worth showing

- **present perfect** for experience: *I've been learning English for ten years.*
- **first conditional** for plans: *If I pass the maturita, I'll apply to…*
- **have to / must / don't have to**: *In Britain pupils have to wear uniforms; we don't have to.*

## Useful phrases

- *What I appreciate about our school is…*
- *I wish we had more…*
- *The main difference between the Czech and British systems is that…*
- *In my experience, the most effective way to learn is…*
- *I'm planning to… / I'm hoping to…*
